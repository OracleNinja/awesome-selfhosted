# Outreach playbook

## The core offer, in one sentence

> "I am establishing a Texas golf-cart sales/service operation and am seeking one
> evaluation/demo unit to establish the product at my location before placing an
> initial inventory order."

Preferred outcome: **$0 evaluation unit, ideally including freight.**
Secondary outcome: **evaluation-unit cost 100% credited toward a first 5-10 unit order.**

Always carry the secondary in the same message as the primary. A manufacturer that
will not give a unit away will often credit one, and you only get that answer if the
question is in front of them when they reply.

## Positioning by audience

**Manufacturer → dealer development.** They care about territory coverage and finding
dealers who will actually stock. Lead with the market you would open, not with what
you want for free. Ask what opens a dealer file.

**Distributor → establishing a local sales channel.** They care about downstream
outlets and moving volume. A placed demo is cheaper for them than losing a territory.
Ask what stocking threshold unlocks a demo, then negotiate the threshold.

**Dealer → dealer/demo partnership or inventory transfer.** They care about floor
space and floor-plan interest on aging units. Make the territory distinction explicit
and early — an unexplained approach from another dealer reads as competitive scouting.
Ask what they are trying to clear.

**Wholesaler → establishing a wholesale relationship.** They care about account volume
and moving non-current stock. Ask for the price sheet, the MOQ, what is sitting in
overstock, and what they need to open an account.

## What makes a message work

- **Reference something specific and real about them**, with the source. The system
  requires at least two evidenced facts before it will render a draft. That threshold
  exists because the alternative is mail-merge, and mail-merge is why these emails get
  ignored.
- **Be concrete about the business as it is.** A new operation asking honestly does
  better than a new operation pretending to be established — and the pretence collapses
  the moment they ask for a dealer number.
- **Label projections as projections.** "I plan to stock 6 units" is credible. "We move
  40 units a year" from a business that has never sold one is a lie that ends the
  relationship when discovered.
- **Make the ask small and bounded.** One unit, not a container. Name what you give
  back: evaluation feedback, photos, display presence, in-market service.
- **Widen what they can say yes to.** Cosmetic blemish, freight damage, non-current
  model year, cancelled order — all acceptable for an evaluation unit. Say so. It turns
  "we have nothing" into "well, there is one thing."
- **Ask one closing question** that is easy to answer: *what would you need from me to
  open a dealer file?*

## Follow-up angles (never repeat the ask)

| Day | Angle | The move |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Specific inventory question | Ask about non-current, cancelled, overstock, blemished units |
| 7 | Program mechanics | Ask what their program *requires* rather than what they can do for you |
| 14 | Right person | Ask who handles dealer development; invite a forward |
| 30 | Close the loop | Say it is the last note, ask to be kept on file, leave gracefully |

The engine refuses to reuse an angle on the same target. Four touches is the ceiling;
after day 30, stop.

## Handling the common replies

- *"Are you an authorized dealer?"* → **The truth: not yet, applying now.** Never imply
  otherwise. Pivot to what onboarding requires.
- *"We don't give free units."* → Immediately offer the credited structure. Ask what
  order size unlocks a demo.
- *"Here's the price."* → Do not accept. Get the full landed cost itemized, then walk
  the concession ladder. Route to the human.
- *"Send your license/EIN."* → Only documents flagged `approved_for_sharing`. Missing
  ones become a human task.
- *"Territory is covered."* → Ask for a referral to a distributor or a dealer with
  aging inventory, then close the loop.
- Anything asking for money before verification → stop, flag the company, hand to the
  human.
